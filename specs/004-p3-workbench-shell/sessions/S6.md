## The one consequential design call in your scope

**T050 puts the scorecard and the canvas in two different regions of the shell.**
`Drawer` and `CenterView` are siblings under `WorkbenchShell`, so hovering a
metric in one has to reach blocks in the other. Where that state lives is yours
to decide, and it decides the shape of T046's tests.

Three things constrain it:

- **Neither side may read its own copy of anything.** FR-023's rule is why the
  matrix and the table agree, and the same rule applies to a highlight: the
  scorecard names *which* blocks, and the canvas draws them, with one definition
  of the set.
- **The highlight is not a fifth encoding channel.** The block's four channels —
  fill, edge-bar and hatch, weapon chip, gender prefix — and their degradation
  order at 64/28/14px are a settled contract with 41 cases behind them. A
  highlight must sit outside them and must not disturb the degradation order.
- **`MatrixCanvas` already takes what it draws as props.** `CenterView` hands it
  `schedule` and `findings` from one committed object, and the canvas falls back
  to the store only when mounted bare. A `highlight` prop follows that precedent
  — a store slice would work too, but say which you chose and why.

Decide it before T046, not during T050. A test written against a guess is a test
rewritten later.

## The things most likely to go silently wrong

**1. Six times in this feature a green suite has said nothing about a rule it
appeared to cover.** Read S5's handoff section on this. The shapes, all found by
mutation rather than by a red test:

- a rule that only manifested after a timer, so a synchronous assertion never saw it
- an assertion its own fixture satisfied before the code under test ran
- every fixture reaching a behaviour by a path that made the assertion vacuous
- an expectation computed from the same formula as the implementation
- **a fixture that already satisfied the branch under test**, so a fallback path
  never ran and its deletion changed nothing
- **an assertion aimed at the middle of a target**, where an offset of 38px was
  still comfortably inside it

Assert literals. For every assertion ask what mutation of the source would make
it fail, and if the answer is none, strengthen it. **Mutate the load-bearing
lines after you are green, confirm a failure, revert, and report the table.**
S5's test review ran as 40 executed mutations against a scratchpad copy of the
worktree rather than as reading, and that is what found six real holes — it is a
method worth repeating.

**2. Findings fixtures do not resemble what the engine emits.** Day-level
warnings carry an **empty `competition_id`** and attach to no competition at all
(`analysis.ts` `STRIP_CONTENTION` on `Phase.CAPACITY`). Per-competition warnings
come on `Phase.POOLS`, `Phase.CUT` and `Phase.FLIGHTING`. T045's "finding counts
by severity" will read right against a hand-built fixture and wrong against the
real thing unless the fixture matches. Dump `selectDerivedFindings` for your
actual fixture before writing the expectation.

**3. `computePoolStructure` (`src/engine/pools.ts:25`) throws for
`fencerCount <= 1`**, and `initialAnalysis` calls it for every selected
competition regardless of placement. A low fencer count crashes the render rather
than raising a finding. Use `strips_total` when you need an invalid config, and
`0` versus a small non-zero value are not interchangeable.

**4. `CenterView` debounces its commit by `CENTER_SETTLE_MS` (150ms) and
suppresses it entirely while any finding is ERROR.** The drawer is deliberately
*not* debounced — FR-008 has findings and metrics follow the store per keystroke
while the centre waits. **The scorecard is drawer-side, so it follows the live
store, not the committed model.** T050's highlight then crosses that boundary:
the metric is live and the blocks are committed. Decide what that means and say
so — it is the kind of divergence that reads as a bug the first time an organizer
sees a metric move while the highlighted blocks do not.

**5. jsdom does no layout.** `getBoundingClientRect()` returns zeros, `hidden`
does not remove an element from queries, and jsdom 26 ships **no `PointerEvent`
constructor** — `fireEvent.pointerMove(el, {clientX})` degrades to a bare `Event`
with `clientX` undefined. Dispatch `new MouseEvent('pointermove', {clientX,
clientY, bubbles: true})` instead. Radix portals **two** copies of tooltip
content, so any `data-tooltip-field` read needs `.first()`.

**6. S1's `localStorage` guard in `src/test-setup.ts` is not redundant.** It
installs an in-memory `Storage` shim only when the ambient `localStorage` lacks
working methods — a Node ≥24 workaround that is a no-op on CI's Node 22. T051
depends on it. Do not delete it because your machine is green.
